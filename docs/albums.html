Album Reviews

Worst Girl in America

Crank It! The third album from the self proclaimed “Worst Girl in America”, Slayyyter, combines addictive beats, the rush of a night out, and introspection, making it an immediate classic pop album while retaining her own unique style. In the age of increased examination of the merit of “nepo babies”, Slayyyter brings an authentically honest and raw expression of her artistic vision. The music video to the 4th track on the album, “Old Technology”, was reportedly made with a zero dollar budget, utilizing a friend’s camera and outfits Slayyyter made herself. The fireworks in the “Beat Up Chanels” music video were shot in a twenty minute window on the Fourth of July to achieve the effect despite not having the budget for their own pyrotechnics. The album progresses through feelings of ecstasy, heartache, adrenaline, and freedom culminating in the track “Brittany Murphy”. After feeling as though we’ve run hand in hand with her through a haze of clubs and hangovers, Slayyyter gives her listeners directions to follow upon her death in the planning of her funeral. In a tribute to the late actress Brittany Murphy, Slayyyter puts forth an incredibly raw perspective on her party girl persona. For now, the party rages on, and pop fans can join Slayyyter on her electrifying journey through the night.

Eusexua

FKA Twigs’ third album, Eusexua, has seemingly descended from the cosmos into your latest playlist. The album cover itself suggests other-worldy ambience, and the album certainly delivers with electronic sounds and beats like you've never heard. While the album features many very different sounding tracks, it is clear they all reside on Planet Twigs, as each track makes you feel compelled to move while singing lyrics that cut to the bone. My personal favorite is "Sushi", which is supposedly an homage to her amazing dancers that perform with her. All I know is that when I listen to this album "in my head I have the best time".

Euro-Country

CMAT is by no means a shy artist. Her latest album, "Euro-Country", is both loud and fun, while also bringing shatteringly raw and often relatable lyrics. CMAT has discussed her worship of Dolly Parton from a young age, and this album's title speaks to that influence in her artistic sound. She has even made a point to try as many Dolly Parton affiliated meals as she can while touring in the States. Euro-Country is comprised of just that - CMAT's Irish traditional sound combined with American country influences. With her voice as powerful as ever, CMAT sings and joyfully dances her way through an album that is redefining music genres as we know them.
